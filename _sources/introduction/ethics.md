## Why is ethics important? 

As researchers, we have a responsibility to ensure that we treat participants, who are providing their own time to take part in research, with care and respect. We have a duty to respect and protect the well-being, rights, and dignity of our participants.

## Treatment of participants

✓ Participation should be entirely voluntary

-	**No-one is obliged** to participate in a study. We should collect no data from any participants who refuse to take part. 
-	Do not investigate children, patients, elderly, or other groups with **enhanced vulnerabilities** without consent of the ethics committee. 
-	Participants need to be told that they will **not benefit** from participation. (Any payment is only a compensation.) 
-	**No-one should be discriminated against** (i.e., there is no selection bias in recruitment). Tell participants from the outset who you are looking for and who will not be able to participate (for what reason?).
    
✓ Participation should be fully informed 

-	**Text** explaining the study needs to be well structured, and easily understandable. Use short sentences, written specifically for the participants, with no technical jargon ("Fach-Jargon")! In some cases, you may want to consider showing a flowchart to illustrate the procedures. 
-	**Why** is the study being conducted? Provide some scientific background to participants. Do benefits outweigh the costs (i.e. costs for participants, not yours!)?
-	**Who** is doing the study? Provide details on who participants can contact to get more information and answer any questions.
-	**What** will be done? Provide step-by-step details on what will happen in the study. 
-	**How long** does it take?
-	**Minimal deception** - Weigh costs against benefits. If deception is personally relevant (e.g. performance feedback, personality trait scores, societal norms), then by all means ask the ethics committee. Take extra care when debriefing, and fully reveal any information that was not provided before the experiment. Do not apologise, but explain why the deception was necessary and unavoidable. Ask participants afterwards if they still agree to have their data stored and analyzed, and invite them to question or discuss the procedures. You can provide space for participants to provide free response (e.g., textbox in a survey) after the study to identify any adverse issues that results from any deception.
-	**Minimal burden** - In terms of effort, but also emotion, we should minimise unnecessary burdens on the participant. If there is any doubt, ask the ethics committee and/or consider a “trigger warning” or “disclaimer” before the experiment. 
-	Always tell participants that they can **quit** at any time without providing reasons. Provide compensation (VPM, monetary compensation, etc.) in proportion to the time that they have spent participating. 
- Provide details on any **risks** of participation.

✓ Participation requires consent

- Consent should be **active**, in the form of a checkbox or signature, rather than passive.

## Data protection

We are responsible for ensuring that participants' data is protected, in accordance with laws like The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).

✓ Differentiate anonymous from pseudonymous from person-related acquisition and storage of data (**Anonymisiert** versus **pseudonymisiert** versus **personenbezogene** Datenerhebung und -speicherung).

-	If anonymous and without notable risk, no ethical approval is necessary.
-	If pseudonomized, provide information on where the code list is stored and how it is protected and when it will be deleted (needs to be asap). Pseudonyms need to be really strong, but note that participants often make errors in generating codes themselves. 
-	Inform participants that after deletion of person-related data, data cannot be deleted anymore even if requested. 
-	Any personal data, including video or audio or handwritten material, needs to be especially protected. It needs to be registered and monitored by the [Datenschutzbeauftragte of Goethe University](https://www.uni-frankfurt.de/111064324/Datenschutz). You also need a statement from the ethics committee (visit the [institute’s website](https://www.uni-frankfurt.de/141179107/Die_Gremien_des_Fachbereich_05) for more information on the local ethics committee). 
-	Personal data include names, addresses, email addresses, matrikel numbers; any information that might possibly enable someone to identify the person.

✓	Participants are considered the owners of any data they provide.

- If participants request it, they can **access their own data**.
- Participants can also request **deletion of their own data**.
- If sharing data online (e.g., on LIFOS), you should mention any deadline for requesting data deletion.
